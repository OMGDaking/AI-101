# Project 2 brief — excerpt supplied September 24, 2026

> Platform note (October 15, 2026): this is a preserved course excerpt. The user now runs workflows in Codex; tool names in the excerpt are original source wording.

Learn that good AI work is mostly prep. You find a problem in your own daily process, design a workflow to solve it, and build that workflow into a folder of context and directions. Then you point a model at the folder and let it do the work you designed.

## Overview

In Project 1, you set up your space: Obsidian, the Claudian plugin, and a model that can read your vault. In Class 3, you used that setup to have a model build a scheduled sync of your vault to GitHub. That sync was a workflow. Before the model could build it, you had to tell it where your vault lives, which repo to use, and that the repo stays private. That was the prep.

Project 2 asks you to do that prep on purpose, for a problem you choose.

Orchestration means directing a model through a workflow you designed. You prepare the context and directions. The model does the steps. You check every result and decide what happens next.

Here is an example problem: “I receive documents to review from my instructor. How can I organize them and create a study guide without compromising my own thinking?” Your problem will come from your own process.

Compare each run against your intent. Where the output falls short, a misunderstanding, a vague direction, or a step you now see differently, you log it, decide what to change, and fix your directions. Those decisions are what you are graded on.

## Words to Know

Workflow: a series of steps that turns an input into an output.
Context: everything the model needs to know to do the job well: your goal, your steps, your rules, and examples.
Canvas map: an Obsidian Canvas where each box (node) is one step, connected by arrows in order.
Start file: 00-start-here.md, the first file the model reads. It states the goal, points to the map, and lists the rules.
Step file: one Markdown file per step that explains what the step does, what goes in, what good looks like, and how you will check it.
Run: pointing the model at your folder in Claudian and having it follow your start file.
Intent gap: the difference between what you meant and what the model actually did.

## Materials and Skills Applied

The design process: intent, research, prototype, feedback, and iteration.
An AI-free map of your own process.
Real, named sources and a source verification log.
Obsidian Canvas, Markdown, and Claudian, all set up in Project 1. No new tools, installs, or purchases.
An AI Use Log and a decision note that record the gaps you found and what you changed.
Peer feedback and one visible revision.
