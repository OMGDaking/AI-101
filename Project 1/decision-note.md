# Decision note — neon monoliths

I kept the fourth output, [AI101_P1_Krug_Dakota_Poster.png](artifacts/AI101_P1_Krug_Dakota_Poster.png), because it makes the context kit visible in the image’s structure, not just its color. The figure sits on the left in a dark-wine coat, the right side becomes a single teal monolith, and a magenta field creates the empty visual pressure between them. The vertical ridges on the face and hair are the most specific reference driven feature: they translate the ribbed glass portrait into a rule rather than copying its subject. The final still feels like a neon city, but it has enough open space to read as a poster at a distance.

I rejected the first output, [round-1-baseline.png](Image%20Rounds/round-1-baseline.png), despite it following the literal format of a square, three-color, no-text image. It chose a soft yellow desk scene, a cat, and a student’s laptop. Those choices are exactly the model’s default association with “student” and “pin-up,” not the rainy, architectural, nocturnal feeling of my references. The comparison makes the value of the kit clear: the shared prompt alone did not contain any of my visual preferences.

The largest change between the first and final outputs is the composition. The baseline spreads several small domestic objects across the frame. The final uses only a figure, one building, a field of sky, and a narrow set of reflections. The steering move that mattered most was the third round’s keep/drop instruction: keep the rain-city energy and long coat, but drop the crowded city canyon and cables. That gave the final round a simple foundation. From there, changing the full-body low-angle pose to a waist-up left-third portrait separated the coat from the architecture and made the ribbed face the focal point.
