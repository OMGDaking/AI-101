## My problem

I receive course readings, assignment briefs, and instructor instructions. How can I organize them into a clear study guide and task list without letting AI decide what I understand or write my answers for me?

## What I receive

Assigned readings, project briefs, instructions posted for class, and my own class notes. These documents contain ideas to learn, requirements to follow, and questions I need to answer.

## My steps now

1. Open the assigned reading or brief and read the original document.
2. Write down the main ideas and assignment requirements in my notes.
3. Compare my notes with the original to find missing details or confusing instructions.
4. Put the notes into a study guide with a separate list of tasks and any stated deadlines.
5. Use the guide to test my understanding, return to passages I cannot explain, and choose what to work on next.

## Where it breaks down

In this proposed process, steps 2–4 are the slowest part. Ideas from readings and requirements from briefs get mixed together. I have to reopen documents to check whether a note is a concept to learn or something I must submit. If I shorten a passage too much, I can lose a detail or mistake my interpretation for an instruction.

## What a good output looks like

A study guide has separate sections for key ideas, assignment tasks, and open questions. Every key idea and requirement includes a source title and page number or section heading so I can check it. Deadlines appear only when the original states them; otherwise the guide says “not stated.” It includes three practice questions I answer myself before checking the reading. A partner can trace each task back to the source and identify what remains unclear.

## What AI must not decide

AI must not decide whether I understand a reading, supply my personal interpretation as if it were mine, or write the answers I submit. I choose what needs more study and answer the practice questions myself. I check every requirement and deadline against the original before using the task list.

## Material I will run the workflow on

The Project 2 assignment brief or a course practice brief, with any private information removed. I will exclude grades, financial or health information, and other people's personal information.

## Research question

For college students working with assigned readings, how does retrieval practice compare with rereading for remembering and explaining the material?

## Evidence that would answer it

A peer-reviewed study or research review comparing retrieval practice and rereading, using delayed recall or comprehension tests with college students, would help me decide how to build practice questions into the guide.
