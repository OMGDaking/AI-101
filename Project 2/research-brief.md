## Research question

For college students working with assigned readings, how does retrieval practice compare with rereading for remembering and explaining the material?

## Short answer

The answer depends on when recall is tested: the prose study found an immediate advantage for restudying but a delayed advantage for retrieval practice. [Source 1](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/)
The vocabulary study found that continued retrieval after initial learning helped delayed recall more than continued study alone. [Source 2](https://web.mit.edu/educationgroup/HHMIEducationGroup/wp-content/uploads/2011/04/14-Karpicke-Roediger-2008.pdf)
These findings support trying self-testing in my workflow, but the checked claims establish recall effects, not improved personal explanations or benefits from AI-generated guides.

## Claim 1: Timing changes the comparison

### The claim I checked

Retrieval practice is always better than rereading, even immediately.

### What the source says

Roediger and Karpicke's 2006 article abstract reports that repeated study outperformed repeated testing at five minutes. See Source 1 and its exact sentence in the verification log.

### The corrected claim

In these prose experiments, rereading helped immediate recall; retrieval's advantage occurred after a delay.

### Why this matters for my workflow

I should not judge the guide only by how familiar the reading feels immediately afterward.

### The rule, input, or check this supports

In `05-practice-recall.md`, add a later attempt at the questions; this is a workflow choice informed by the finding, not a tested schedule for my classes.

## Claim 2: Keep practicing retrieval after an initial success

### The claim I checked

Continued retrieval after initial learning can benefit delayed recall.

### What the source says

Karpicke and Roediger's 2008 vocabulary experiment supports this claim; see Source 2, printed page 966, and the exact sentence in the verification log.

### The corrected claim

In that experiment, repeated retrieval after initial learning improved delayed vocabulary recall, while repeated study alone did not.

### Why this matters for my workflow

One successful answer should not automatically remove a question from future practice. Applying this to course readings is a proposed adaptation to test, not a result established by the vocabulary experiment.

### The rule, input, or check this supports

In `05-practice-recall.md`, keep previously answered questions available for a later attempt and record my answers before consulting the source.
