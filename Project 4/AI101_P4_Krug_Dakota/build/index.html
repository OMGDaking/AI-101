YOUR FIRST MELODY

Listen. See. Play.

  1. Upload a recording. Choose a song or piano recording up to six minutes and 75 MB. Audio is decoded and analyzed on your device.
  2. Let Keyline make a draft. The model detects multiple notes. Keyline selects a likely melody and generates a simple left-hand bass part. It does not separate studio stems or identify the exact original arrangement.
  3. Choose your difficulty. Easy adds a bass note per bar. Intermediate adds repeated root-and-fifth accompaniment. Change the setting and rebuild without analyzing the audio again.
  4. Play and learn. Hear both hands, slow down, loop, and choose which hand the visual guide follows. Finger positions are starting suggestions, not optimized fingering.
  5. Download it. Get MIDI, editable MusicXML, or printable PDF sheet music. These use an estimated tempo and 4/4 meter; mixed recordings can produce wrong notes, missing notes, and incorrect rhythm.

Both animated hands sit directly on the main keyboard: finger numbers light in time with playback and the hand slides when the practice position changes. Use Moving hand to hide or show the overlay. Lessons use fixed five-note positions. Songs and scores use an adaptive hand span, including octaves; extra sounding voices remain illuminated while the hand follows its current grip. Choose Both hands, Right hand or Left hand, then step through notes or press play. For uploaded melodies, positions and fingers are simple starting suggestions; review them for comfort. Selecting a hand does not separate an audio recording into left and right parts.

Piano sound: Steinway Model B recordings by Evan Mazunik, University of Iowa Electronic Music Studios / Lawrence Fritts. Sample credits. Compact mono sample set, one dynamic layer, with pitch interpolation.
